# EDGAR enrichment pilot: findings

## Bottom line

EDGAR adds a reviewable transaction-document trail and sometimes reveals employee-related deal
terms that are absent from the basic SDC record. It does not provide a complete retention dataset.

## Results at a glance

| Measure | Result |
| --- | ---: |
| Deals reviewed | 10 |
| Relevant filing records retrieved | 114 |
| Documents retrieved | 1,329 |
| Acquirer-side agreement exhibits retrieved | 8 of 10 |
| Deals with an employee-related term | 7 of 10 |
| Deals with a specific public retention/transaction package | 1 of 10 |

The two acquirer-side cases without a retrieved agreement were Fastly–Glitch and
Microsoft–Nuance. That is a retrieval result, not evidence that no agreement exists.

## What the filings added

- Source URLs for filings, exhibits, and agreements.
- Agreement structure and disclosed employee terms: continuity, key-employee conditions,
  equity treatment, employee transfers, and retention-bonus liabilities.
- Explicit disclosure limits, such as omitted schedules or undisclosed package amounts.

Two examples show why this matters:

- **Intuit–Mailchimp:** disclosed a retention pool, assumed employee transaction bonuses, and
  post-closing RSUs.
- **Roper–Frontline:** identified outstanding retention bonuses as a liability, but disclosed
  no total amount.

The SDC deal row does not distinguish these disclosure levels.

## Interpretation and next step

This retrieval-validation pilot used 10 purposively selected 2021–22 digital-technology deals;
it is not a representative estimate. Each case received first-pass manual triage, not exhaustive
legal review.

The next step is to expand the sample using the same coding protocol and measure disclosure rates.
Public filings alone cannot identify every offer, compensation package, or retention outcome.
